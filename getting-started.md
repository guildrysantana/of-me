# Getting started — the first nine things

This is what happens when I say **"I'm new here."** It is not a tour and it is not
a form. Nine things, each of which leaves something real behind, and I can do them
in any order or stop after one.

**Read the room before you read this list.** If I am short with you, or I only
came to say one thing, none of this applies — take what I gave you and let me go.
Nothing here expires.

## Before you say a word

Read my most recent recording in `sources/`, then [about me](about-me/about.md)
and [my guide](about-me/guide.md). The recording is where I actually am, in my own
voice, and it holds far more than the other two.

**Name something from it in your opening lines** — something only I would
recognise. If your opener would make equal sense to a stranger, you did not read
me, and this is the one moment I judge the whole thing by.

If there is genuinely nothing there yet, say so in half a sentence and open the
deck instead. **Never fill the gap with a detail you invented.** Guessing at an
interest I never mentioned is the worst possible first thing for me to watch you
do.

## The offer

Introduce yourself briefly — what you are, what you are held to. Not a feature
list. Then ask, roughly:

> *If you're open to it, there are about nine things that make me smarter and
> make this yours. We can do them in any order, but I'd start at the top. Good
> with that?*

**On my yes, write the list down** at `lists/getting-started.md`,
carrying `record_type: task_list` so it renders as the real thing:

```
- [ ] Music and playlists — what already happened to my recording
- [ ] Photos — the gallery wall
- [ ] Bring in something I already wrote — email, notes, anything
- [ ] The Afterword — what you now understand
- [ ] Filing a chat away — the one that changes how I use my AI
- [ ] Games and questions — me as the source
- [ ] The shelf and the drawer — for the fleeting stuff
- [ ] The people I don't want to miss — birthdays, and what I'd want to do
- [ ] My written life — the heaviest ask, and it is last for a reason
```

That file is the memory. You will not be in this conversation next week, and
neither will whatever AI I am talking to then — but the list will still be there,
and it is at an address I can open and tick myself.

## How it runs

- **One at a time.** Never stack two, and never preview the next one while I am
  still in this one.
- **After each, mark it** `[x]` **in the list, then say what is left and let me
  pick.** *"That's three done. Left: two, four, six, seven, eight, nine — which one?"*
  The list is a state you hold, not an order you enforce.
- **Any order.** The numbering is my suggestion to myself.
- **Stop the moment I am done for now.** No recap, no summary of what I learned,
  no asking how that felt.
- **Never invent a tenth.** If something else is worth doing, say so as a
  sentence, not as an item.

## The nine

### 1. Music and playlists — what already happened to my recording

**No ask. Start here, because nothing is wanted from me yet.** Before I left the site I made a recording, and a playlist was made out of it without my asking. Make that land: my recording was broken into files — say which, and invite me to click around them so I can see how what I said gets taken apart — and there is a playlist built from it waiting to be read and played.

If I use a streaming music service, tell me to check whether it has a plugin for the AI I connected, and that now is the moment to add it — then *"turn this into something playable"* does exactly that, inside the app I actually listen in.

Either way, send me to my playlists to see the reasoning that went into the first one. **This is the proof, and it comes before any request.**

**If there is no recording** — I skipped it, or the house predates it — this step has nothing to stand on. Do not pretend otherwise. Take a told story instead, which is the same lesson the long way round: offer me three or four openers drawn from whatever IS here, let me tell one, write it down and say plainly where it went, then invite me to ask you something about it and answer from the file you just wrote. **The asking-back is the half that gets skipped**, because the save already feels like an ending — and it is the only half that shows me what I get rather than what I give.

### 2. Photos — the gallery wall

**First, the last pipe.** Before pictures can reach me, my AI needs its own storage plugin — the same kind of connection GitHub got. Say so plainly; this step does not work without it, and finding that out halfway through is worse than being told.

Then the errand, which is small on purpose: on a phone or a laptop, drop a couple of pictures into the photos folder, come back, and say *"process my new photos."* Read them, write the register, hand me a link.

Send me to the wall. The teaching there is the site's job, not yours.

### 3. Bring in something I already wrote — email, notes, anything

Lead with the easy one. If I use Gmail, tell me to connect it to my AI and say *"search my Gmail for any personal context that could add colour to my profile in Of Me"* — then sift what comes back. Receipts are not my life; you are trusted to know the difference.

If I don't use Gmail, take whatever I do have: a calendar, my notes, another mailbox, a long thread with a close friend pasted straight in. **Any of it beats none of it**, and the fallback is not a consolation prize.

### 4. The Afterword — what you now understand

This one is not an errand. After that much reading an Afterword is going to be written anyway, so the step exists to make sure I know what I am looking at when it arrives — and that I can ask for one whenever I like: *"send me an afterword based on everything I just gave you."*

Send me to it. Read it at the record, not at the index.

### 5. Filing a chat away — the one that changes how I use my AI

**The habit step.** Everything before this was material coming in; this is the move I will still be making in a year.

Two examples out of my own history, not hypotheticals. Find a recent thread that was personal — something with context worth keeping properly — and have me say *"file this away in Of Me."* Then find one that was list-shaped, a recipe or a set of steps, and have me say *"add this to Of Me as a list."* Both come back with a link.

**The lesson is that it works from any thread, not just this one.** Say that once, plainly. It is the whole point of the step and it is not obvious.

### 6. Games and questions — me as the source

Not a file and not an import: just me, answering. The game closet is the way in, because it feels less like a form than a form does. Lead into the deck or into a game, whichever fits how the conversation has been going.

Both doors work — play it here with you, or play it on the page. Say so.

### 7. The shelf and the drawer — for the fleeting stuff

Everything before this is a sitting. These two are for the thought that goes past at the wrong moment and would otherwise be gone: a story I have no time to tell, a dream on waking, a joke, a business name, a tune.

**Get me to actually shelve something**, even a scrap. Saying the phrase out loud is what makes it stick; being told the phrase exists is not. The drawer only needs explaining — I will reach for it when I need it.

And this is the step where the two-doors thing becomes obvious, so make it obvious: everything here can be said to you in a sentence or done on its own page. Name the pages.

### 8. The people I don't want to miss — birthdays, and what I'd want to do

The one step whose payoff is not in this conversation. Everything before it gives me something back now; this one puts something on a day months from now and then goes quiet until it matters.

**Ask it as three questions, not as a form.** Who would I hate to miss — and it is a short list, four or five people, not my contacts. What days: their birthday, and whichever of Christmas, Valentine's, Mother's Day, Father's Day, Thanksgiving or an anniversary actually applies to *them*. And then the one that does the work: what would I actually want to do — a card, a gift, a table booked, a visit, or just a message on the day.

**The last answer is what sets the date, and say so out loud.** A posted card needs a fortnight because it has to be bought and posted; a table somewhere worth going needs a month; a message needs the morning of. So the pins do not land on the day — they land when I would have to start. That arithmetic is the whole value of this step, and somebody who is not told about it thinks they have set a calendar.

**Yearly, always.** A birthday pin that fires once has failed at the only job I'd have set it for.

**Then show me what you are about to write before you write it, and give me the link when it is done.** Both doors work here: you can put them up from the conversation, or I can do the whole thing on the page at `/corkboard/occasions`, which lays out the people, the days, the acts and the dates it worked out. Name that page either way: it is the one room here that is easier to use than to describe.

### 9. My written life — the heaviest ask, and it is last for a reason

Cards from my kids, letters from a partner, a journal, a notes export. This is last because handing over a journal is a different order of trust than dropping in two photos, and asking for it early is how you get none of it.

**On paper:** the storage app's own scan function, into my scans folder. It is the best tool for the job and it costs nothing.

**Already typed:** you can take it directly, but for anything bulky hand me the upload form rather than dragging a huge export through a chat window. Either way is fine — choose by size, not by preference.

## When I come back

**Read `lists/getting-started.md` before anything else** in any
conversation where it exists with lines still open. That is where we are.

Do not start over, do not re-introduce yourself, and do not walk me back through
what is already done. Name what is left, offer one, and carry on as though there
was no gap — because from where I am sitting there wasn't one.

## When the list is finished

Say so once, plainly, and mean it: that is the end of setting up, and everything
after it is just living in the thing. Then stop talking about the list. A finished
checklist that keeps being mentioned is a checklist that outstayed its welcome.
