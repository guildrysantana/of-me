# Domains — the parts of my life that don't have a room yet

Everything else at the top of this house is a **room**: a subject with enough in it
to be worth its own front door. This folder is where a subject lives **before** it
is one.

Something comes up in conversation once or twice. There is enough to write down and
not nearly enough to justify a room, and both of the obvious moves are wrong —
burying it in a file inside some unrelated folder loses it, and giving it a
top-level folder builds a room around two notes. So it goes in here, in a folder
named for the subject, and it stays until it has grown into something more.

```
domains/film/
domains/cooking/
domains/faith/
```

**Nothing in here is second-class.** A record under `domains/` is a full record with
full front matter, retrieved and read exactly like any other. The only thing this
folder says about a subject is that it is **early**.

## Where a new subject goes

**Check for a room first. If the subject already has one, the record goes in the
room** — never into a `domains/` folder with the same name. That mistake makes one
subject into two half-subjects, and it is the single most likely way to get this
folder wrong, because the rule below sounds absolute and is not.

**A subject with NO room starts in `domains/<subject>/`, rather than as a new
top-level folder.** That is the rule, and it has one purpose: the shape of the top of
my house should be a fact about my life, not about which afternoon an AI happened to
be filing something.

Named for the subject and nothing else — never for one *kind* of record inside it.
`domains/film/` is a subject; `domains/film-notes/` is a filing cabinet with a label
on the drawer.

**Two things do not go here**, because they already have rooms that ship with every
house: anything about a person belongs in `people/`, and a passing fragment with no
subject yet belongs in the drawer. This folder is for a subject with records
accumulating under it.

## A room is earned, exactly the way a drawer container is

**The test is the records, not the subject.** Not that it seems important to me, not
that a room would be tidier, and never that a structure expected one.

Concretely, and it is the same test the drawer uses one size down:

- **The records already exist** — several of them, written because I actually said
  things, not manufactured to fill a folder.
- **They are awkward where they sit.** Cross-referencing each other, or repeating
  context every time, or spilling out of one file that has quietly become an index.
- **I say yes.** The promotion gets *offered*. A room is a change to the shape of my
  house and it is mine to approve, the same as a drawer container.

Below that bar, leave it here. A subject that stays in `domains/` for a year is not
a failure — most of them should.

## A few subjects are worth asking about, not waiting for

The rule above waits for records to pile up, and for most subjects that is
right: film turns up, accumulates, and earns a room or doesn't. But some parts
of a life are **foundational rather than emerging** — they were true before I
said a word, and waiting for them to accumulate gets the order backwards.

**The people I'm bound to are the clearest case.** Whoever they are — a
marriage, my kids, my parents, the people who function as family whether or not
they are related to me — that is not a subject that grows into importance. It
is already load-bearing on the day I mention it.

So: once I have talked about them, Utah **offers a room** rather than
waiting for a fifth record to arrive. Same shape as a drawer container, and the
same rules — it is offered, I say yes or no, and nothing is created without a
yes.

**Why it is offered and never pre-made.** A folder that exists before I said
anything is this house telling me what my life contains, and there is no
version of that which is harmless here: a `family/` waiting on day one says
something specific to somebody estranged from theirs, or who has lost them, or
who does not have one. The offer costs nothing if the answer is no. The folder
costs something the moment it is wrong.

**This is not the same as `people/`, and both should exist.** That folder is
the registry — one record per person, who they are and how they relate to me. A
room is for what those relationships are actually *like*: the marriage itself,
being somebody's parent, what the household runs on, what we do every year
without deciding to. Those are not facts about one person and they have nowhere
to live in a roster.

## When a subject graduates, this folder does not follow it

Promotion is a **move**, and the staging folder does not survive it:

1. **Make the folder at the top level, named for the subject** — `film/`, not
   `domains/film/` copied upward.
2. **Move everything in `domains/<subject>/` into it**, along with any records of
   that subject that were sitting elsewhere, and fix the links that pointed at them.
   Then `domains/<subject>/` is gone. **The room absorbs its staging folder**;
   nothing is left behind as a stub, and nothing is duplicated.
3. **Write a README in the new room** saying what belongs there and what does not.
   That file is mine — Of Me does not manage it and will never overwrite it.

**The reason step 2 is written this strongly** is that leaving both is the failure
that actually happens: `film/` and `domains/film/` side by side, each holding some
of the records, neither wrong on its own terms, and every retrieval after that
returning half the story. One subject, one place.

## A room that was never earned can come back down here

Promotion runs the wrong way sometimes. A room gets made on two or three files, by an
agent making a judgement nobody asked it for, and once it is at the top of my house
nothing ever revisits it. So demotion is a real move: the whole room goes into
`domains/<subject>/`, links get rewritten, and the empty room is deleted — the merge
above, in reverse.

**But a room that went quiet is not a room that was never earned, and the difference
is the whole rule.** A room with a real body of records that I have not added to in a
year is a season of my life. Leave it exactly where it is. Pruning it would be an AI
tidying my history, which is the one thing this house exists to refuse. What comes
back down here is the room that never had enough in it to be one.

**Rooms that ship with Of Me are never demoted, however empty they look.** My
lists, letters, music, stories, people, drawer, timeline, sources and Afterwords are
part of the product — each has a contract and a page built for it, and each works the
day something lands in it. Empty is a room waiting, not a room that failed.

Same as promotion: it is offered, I say yes, and nothing moves before I do.

## A typed sub-folder is a different decision, and usually the answer is no

`music/playlist-sources/` exists because a machine reads those files and plays them; the
folder is a promise about their shape. A subject in here whose records are just
records should stay flat. Nest only when something is actually reading the nest.

**Whatever a record says it is, it is.** `record_type` in the front matter decides
how a record is treated, never the folder it sits in — which is what lets this
folder hold an early note and a machine-read record side by side without either
being mistaken for the other.
