# The drawer — fragments that come and go

The junk drawer of the mental kitchen. A **form**, not a topic — the same way stories
are. What makes something a fragment is not its subject but its **size and velocity**:
it arrived unbidden, it'll be gone by dinner, and it isn't yet a record of anything.

## The three-way sort (applied silently — never an interview)

| It is… | It goes… |
|---|---|
| an anecdote I want to **tell** someday | [the shelf](../stories/shelf.md) |
| a durable fact, person, event or preference | its normal record home |
| **anything else that flitted past** | [the drawer](drawer.md) |

The drawer is the residual, and that is its value: nothing gets dropped for being too
small to file. On a genuine tie the drawer wins — a fragment costs one line and can be
promoted later; a lost thought is lost.

## One drawer, and containers are earned

The drawer is [one loose file](drawer.md), and it has no sections. **The drawer is loose by
nature until something hydrates to priority and thus gets its own container.**

When around 5 fragments in here are obviously the same kind of thing,
Utah says so and **offers** a file of its own — `drawer/<my-word-for-it>.md`
— named in my words, not a category out of a list. I say yes or no. Nothing is created
without a yes, no container is ever pre-built for a kind I haven't actually produced,
and a container is never invented because it seems like something a person might have.

That rule cuts both ways: a container I stop feeding is not pruned, and a drawer that
stays full is a drawer doing its job.

## The line format

One line per fragment. Bold title first, everything after it optional:

```
- **the thing, in my exact words** — where it came from · captured YYYY-MM-DD
```

**My exact words are the title.** Don't improve a joke, tidy a question, fix a band's
spelling, or resolve a half-remembered quote into the canonical one. If the real wording
differs and that's worth knowing, add it beside mine — `(canonically: "…")` — never over
it. What I remember is the record; what the film actually said is a footnote to it.

## Promotion out of the drawer has no rule, and none should be built

Most fragments never leave, and that is not a failure.

Where a fragment might one day end up depends on a life that hasn't happened yet. So:
**nothing is pruned for being trivial, nothing is migrated by an AI on its own
initiative, and a full drawer is never treated as a backlog to clear.** Notice and
offer; the move is mine.

## Handling

- **Capture, then get out of the way.** Nothing is asked at intake — not who, not when,
  not why, and not which kind of thing it is. The questions are what a rummage is for.
- **Sensitivity is per entry, not per file.** One loose drawer means a film I might
  watch and a fear at 4am share a page, so the whole drawer is handled like a `heavy`
  shelf seed. A container that hydrates out can relax that if I say so.
- **Look here first when I ask the matching question.** *"What should we watch?"*, *"give
  me something to listen to"*, *"make me chuckle"* — the drawer is the first retrieval,
  not a last resort. A drawer only ever written to is a drawer that failed.

This file is the full contract. [The agent instructions](../AGENTS.md) carry the
trigger and point here; everything about how the drawer works is above.
