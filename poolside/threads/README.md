# Threads

**One file per scene, named for the scene** — the slug the scene's own file
uses, so [the first morning](../first-morning.md) has a thread of the same
name in here. Each is the memory of that conversation once the chat window
it happened in has closed. None of them exist until somebody sits down.

**This folder is mine and nothing regenerates it.** Updating my tuning rewrites
the contracts upstairs; it never touches a word in here.

## Why a scene needs one

A scene is not run once. I come back to it, and each sitting is supposed to
start where the last one stopped and go further out. Without a thread that is
impossible: the next conversation opens by asking me something I answered in
June, and the impression that anybody was listening ends right there.

## What an entry looks like

Append one per sitting, newest last. Write it AS THE SITTING GOES, not at the
end — a window closed early takes everything unwritten with it.

    ## 2026-08-22

    **Asked:** what you actually put to me, in your words.
    **I said:** the key phrases, VERBATIM, in quotation marks — not everything,
      the two or three lines that carry it.
    **The gist:** what I was getting at, in a sentence.
    **Where we stopped:** the thing left hanging, so the next sitting has a door.

**Not a transcript, and not a summary.** Every word is too much; a summary
alone is too little. The summary is yours and the phrases are mine, and in six
months only one of those is evidence about me.

**Never a verdict.** A thread holds what was asked and what I said. It does not
score my positions, rank them, or decide what they add up to — and nothing
cites it as proof of anything about me.

## This is not the record

The thread is how a scene remembers itself. A value, a belief or a rule I live
by that came out of one is a record of its own in `about-me/reflections/`, with
its line in [the questions I have answered](../../about-me/questions.md). The
thread is the conversation; the record is my life.
