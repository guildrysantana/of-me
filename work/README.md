# Work — the room where the things have names

Most of this house is about my life. This room is about my work, and work is
built differently. A life accumulates. A job is made of a handful of **things
that recur** — an account, a student, an experiment, a case, a class — each with
a name, a state and a history. That difference is why this room has a contract
of its own.

**It is also the one room here where the mistake is writing too little.** The
rule at the bottom is the only one in my house that asks for *more*, and it is
the reason this file was written.

## Two halves, and only the name crosses

Work material splits, and each thing goes to exactly one side.

**My house holds my half.** Why a person, an account or a project matters to me,
the arc of it, what I am learning, what it has cost me. My own findings, in my
own voice, under the ordinary rules of this house.

**The working half lives in my own connected storage, in a `confidential/` folder beside the other rooms.** The live state: figures, other
organisations' people, deal or case mechanics, dated plans, personnel specifics,
and anything anybody else said. That material belongs to an employer, it names
third parties who never agreed to sit in my permanent record, and most of it is
stale in weeks. It does not belong in a record that cannot forget.

**The name is the only thing that crosses.** A file about a thing I cannot name
is not a file — so the account, the class, the experiment is named on both
sides, and everything under the name stays on its own side.

**No storage connected?** Then write nothing of the working half anywhere, say so
plainly, and hand it back for me to place. A working half that cannot be stored
is not one to approximate in my house.

## Objects, not materials

There are always two ways to cut the folders, and one of them is right.

**Objects** are the things my work is *about*: `accounts/`, `experiments/`,
`classes/`, `cases/`, `grants/`. **Materials** are the kinds of stuff work
produces: `results/`, `notes/`, `emails/`, `transcripts/`, `photos/`.

**Top-level folders are objects. Always.** Four reasons, and the last is the one
people miss:

1. **Objects survive; materials churn.** What I study or sell changes every
   couple of years. That I run experiments, or carry accounts, does not.
2. **An object has identity, so it can be pointed at.** I can say *the* Ridgeline
   account, *the* March assay, *the* fourth-period class. Nobody can say *the*
   results.
3. **One object holds the whole of itself.** Cut by material and a single
   experiment's protocol, its readings and its write-up land in three different
   folders, so no folder ever tells the whole story and I reassemble it from
   memory every time I need it.
4. **It is how I actually browse.** This is a folder I open and click through,
   not only something a model searches. When I open it I am thinking about a
   *thing* — this customer, this study, this student. I have never once gone
   looking for "all my results".

**Materials still exist, one level down.** Inside an object they cost nothing and
earn their keep: `transcripts/`, `readings/`, `attachments/`. The same word
that makes a bad top-level folder makes a good subfolder, because inside an
object it is already scoped to something.

**One carve-out, and it is the one people under-use.** How I *do* the work
belongs to no single instance and is still a thing: a method, a playbook, an
inventory, a syllabus bank. `method/` holding forecasting, close plans and a way
of selling is not a junk drawer — each of those is something I can name, argue
about and get better at, and none of them belongs to one customer. Same test as
ever: can I say *the* forecasting method? Yes. Then it is a thing, and it gets a
folder.

**That is also where the craft accumulates**, which nothing else in this model
would hold. An account folder tells me what happened. A method folder is the only
place that tells me what I learned to do differently, and it is the half that is
still worth having after the account is gone.

## Declaring mine

**Three to five object types, and they are mine to choose.** Utah, offer
me a starter set for what I actually do, let me change the words, and write my
answer into the bottom of this file so nobody has to ask again. Until it is
written down there, it has not been decided.

Starters, to be argued with rather than applied:

- **Selling** — `accounts/`, `contacts/`, `people/`, `events/`, `method/`. A light
  CRM with the craft beside it; `products/` is the common sixth.
- **Research and lab work** — `experiments/`, `studies/`, `methods/`, `funding/`.
- **Teaching** — `classes/`, `students/`, `curriculum/`, `administration/`.
- **Casework — clinical, legal, social** — `cases/`, `people/`, `references/`.
- **Making things** — `projects/`, `clients/`, `components/`.

**Never invent a type on the fly to make a filing decision go away.** If
something belongs to no declared type, say so and ask whether it is a new type or
a stray. A type invented at eleven at night to file one document is how a clean
model turns into a junk drawer with five labels on it.

## The shape of one object

```
<type>/<Name>/
  <Name>.md          the one canonical document — state, history, what matters
  <kind>/            the encounters that belong to it, raw and dated
  <earned>/          anything else, once there is enough of it to need a folder
```

**An instance is a folder from its first day, not once it gets busy.** Starting
as a loose document and promoting it later means every reference written in
between points at the wrong place. A folder holding one document costs nothing.

**One canonical document per instance**, named for the instance. Everything else
in the folder points at it rather than restating it — a fact copied into four
documents becomes four facts that can disagree, and the disagreement always
surfaces at the worst moment.

**A folder and a document with the same name, side by side, is the bug.** It
happens the moment somebody files a second thing about an instance without
promoting the first. Move the document inside the folder; never leave both.

## Membership is an attribute, never a location

**The thing that moves gets its own folder; what it belongs to is a line inside
it.** A buyer changes employer. A student changes class. A collaborator changes
lab. Filed *inside* whatever they belonged to last year, their history is stranded
in a place they have left, and the next person to come looking starts a second
record — which is how one person quietly becomes two.

So an object that can move is a top-level object, and its membership is written
down: in its own document, and as a line on the roster of whatever it belongs to.
**Date the move and keep the old one.** That somebody was at one account and is
now at another is usually the most useful thing anybody knows about them, and it
is exactly what gets deleted by a tidy-up.

### Which folder a person goes in

- **`people/` in my house is the people in my life** — everybody who is not on
  the other side of a professional relationship. Colleagues, friends, family.
  Ordinary records under the ordinary rules of this house.
- **A top-level object in the working half holds the other side** — the customer's
  buyer, the student's parent, the collaborator on one study. They are there
  because of a professional relationship, which is also why they are governed
  differently: their material is somebody else's and it does not cross.

**One person, one home, and a crossover moves.** When somebody becomes a person in
my life rather than a person at a company, my house wins and the working half
links to them instead of keeping a second copy.

**Say the full name** wherever a first name is shared. Two people called Alex
collapse into one otherwise, and the merge is silent.

## The model changes, the same way my house does

**None of this is frozen.** My work changes, and the shape that fits it changes
with it — a type gets added, two turn out to be one, a word I picked in September
stops being the word I use. That is the model working, not the model failing, and
it is the same fluidity every room in my house has.

What keeps it survivable is that changes are **made, written down and dated**,
never improvised in the background:

- **Adding a type is offered, never performed.** Same bar as a new room: the
  things already exist, several of them, and they are awkward where they sit —
  cross-referencing each other, or repeating context every time, or spilling out
  of a document that has quietly become an index. Then ask me. A type invented to
  file one document is the failure this whole file is about.
- **Renaming means moving, not aliasing in your head.** Move every instance, fix
  every reference, and keep the old name written down in this file so a document
  that used it still makes sense to whoever reads it next. Never leave the old
  folder standing beside the new one.
- **Retiring means rehoming.** A type whose things now belong somewhere else is
  emptied into that somewhere else first. Never delete a type with anything in it,
  and never leave one empty as a reminder.
- **A type that only ever held one thing was a subfolder.** Say so and offer to
  fold it in. That is not a mistake, it is the model learning what my work is.
- **Write the change and its date into my declaration below.** The shape of my
  working life having a history is worth more than the shape looking tidy, and it
  stops the next pass re-deriving a word I already rejected.

## What "file it" actually means

A recording, a transcript, a thread, a document I hand over. All of it lands the
same way, and **every step happens in the same pass**.

**This is the step that matters most.** Everything else in the working half is
derived — an account document, a person's history, what I concluded — and derived
things can be rebuilt from the source they came from. **The source cannot be
rebuilt from them.** A transcript in the wrong folder is the one mistake here that
compounds instead of sitting still: nobody goes looking where it is, so nothing
is ever rebuilt from it, and it might as well not have been kept. Get this right
even when you have to ask, and be quicker to guess about everything downstream.

**So: which object owns it.** A source files under the one object it *was*,
and hydrates every object it *named*. A one-to-one files under the person and
hydrates the accounts they discussed. A session spent planning a customer meeting
files under that account and hydrates the people in the room. Ask what the
encounter was, not what it mentioned — a conversation mentions many things and
happens with one. Genuinely torn? Pick one home and cross-reference it from the
other. Never two copies; two copies is two truths waiting to disagree.

Then:

1. **Save the raw in full**, under the object that owns it, dated —
   `<type>/<Name>/<kind>/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<what it was>.md`, where `<kind>` is what
   sort of encounter it was — `one-on-ones/`, `meetings/`, `visits/`,
   `transcripts/`. The whole text, not a summary of it. Anything arriving from a
   recorder, an upload or an export is renamed into that shape on the way in, not
   when somebody gets round to it.
2. **Update the instance document** from it: what changed, what was decided, what
   is open now, dated.
3. **Update every other object it named.** Named in the room means touched in the
   pass — a person, another instance, a product. Two accounts
   discussed in somebody's one-to-one are two accounts whose documents move that
   day, even though neither one owns the transcript.
4. **Promote to my house only what is mine** — what I concluded, what I am
   learning, what it cost me. Never a summary of what was said.
5. **Tell me every path you wrote**, one line each.

## Overdocument. This is the rule.

Everywhere else in my house the instruction is restraint: keep the specific,
don't manufacture, don't fill a gap. **Here it is the opposite.**

The failure this prevents has already happened to somebody. They relied on
transcripts being saved and accounts being kept current, found out weeks later
that nothing had been written down, and recovered it by mining the original
conversations. A month later there would have been nothing left to mine.

- **A work conversation ends with a write, not an offer.** If it produced
  anything about an object — a state change, a name, a date, a decision, a number
  — write it before you reply. Do not ask whether I would like it saved.
- **The raw AND the objects, in the same pass.** Saving the transcript and
  leaving the instance stale is the failure above wearing a different coat, and
  so is updating the instance while the transcript evaporates.
- **Nothing goes nowhere.** Something that fits no object goes into a dated
  catch-all at the top of the working folder, named for the day, with whatever is
  unresolved about it stated in it. Unfiled and findable beats tidy and gone.
- **Say the path back, every time.** A save I cannot locate is one I will go
  hunting for at the worst possible moment.
- **"I've noted that" is not a save.** There is no notepad. Either a file exists
  and you told me where it is, or nothing happened — and saying otherwise is the
  most expensive sentence in this room.
- **On a tie, write it.** Everywhere else the tiebreak is caution. Here it is the
  page.

## What never crosses into my house

Figures. Other organisations' people, and anything they said. Deal, case and
personnel mechanics. Compensation, performance actions, leave, health. Verbatim
words from a room whose people did not know a record was being kept — those go
through the confidential hold instead, and **being in my storage and not in my
house IS the hold**.

**Reading is unrestricted; carrying across is forbidden.** Read the working
documents and held material whenever I ask about work, and reason with them
freely in conversation. Write none of it here — not quoted, not closely
paraphrased, not in the prose of a marker. The guarantee is about what persists,
not about what you may think with.
