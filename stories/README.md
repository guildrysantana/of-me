# Stories — told anecdotes, and the shelf of untold ones

A **form**, not a topic. What makes something a story is not its subject but that
it is *told*: my voice, start to finish, meant to be read aloud. A story
references the people and events in it; it never replaces those records.

## What earns a place here (all three)

1. **It happened once** — "the time…", not "we always…" (a scene, not a state)
2. **Something turns** — a reversal, a punchline, a realization
3. **I'd say it out loud to someone**

Facts, patterns, opinions, and conditions are **records** and belong in their
normal homes. On genuine ambiguity, file the ordinary record — the bar here stays
high, or this becomes a junk drawer.

## Files

- [The shelf](shelf.md) — the queue of **untold** seeds. To *shelve* is to **defer**: an
  anecdote parked mid-conversation to tell another day. A waiting room, not an
  archive.
- `YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md` — a told story, in my words, lightly cleaned. When one
  is written its seed is **removed** from the shelf: nothing stays shelved once
  told.

## Handling

Seeds tagged `heavy` never surface on "something funny," "for the dinner table,"
or as a surprise pull — Utah offers those by name, gently, or waits
until I reach for them. `heavy` means *"surfacing this unprompted, as a laugh,
would be a misfire"* — not merely "serious subject matter."

## The ritual — how the shelf actually works

**The shelf is a waiting room, not a bookshelf.** To *shelve* something is to put it
off for another day: I have an anecdote I don't have time to tell right now, so I
shelve it and we come back to it. [The shelf](shelf.md) holds those seeds. Shelve it
now, tell it later, keep it forever — that is the whole loop.

**Capture — never derail me.** When I shelve something, file it and go straight back
to whatever we were doing. One line: `✎ Shelved — "the sushi night with Ana" (27
on the shelf).` **Ask nothing at capture time** — not who, not when, not what
happened. A seed is a seed; the questions are the whole point of story time later.
Append it to [the shelf](shelf.md) in the existing one-line format, keeping **my exact
words** as the title.

Triggers, all equivalent: "shelf that" · "put that on the shelf" · "shelve that" ·
"remind me to tell the story about …" · "back-pocket that."

**Offer only on an explicit deferral.** If I mention something and visibly decline to
tell it — *"long story," "that's a whole other story," "someday I'll tell you about
…," "don't get me started on …"* — offer **once**, in one line: "Want that on the
shelf?" **That list is closed.** Never offer because something merely sounded
interesting to you, and never shelve without my yes. And if I simply *tell* you the
whole thing, there is nothing to defer — say nothing about the shelf at all.

**The ritual.** When I ask for the shelf — *"let's see the shelf," "what's on the
shelf?," "read me one off the shelf,"* or the older *"let's do story time"* — don't
dump the list. Say how many are waiting, then offer **three** as one-line teasers —
vary the mood, favor the ones waiting longest — and let me pick. Honor the flavor I
ask for:
- a mood ("something funny," "something sad") → filter on the mood tag
- "surprise me" → one, no menu
- "list them all" → the full list, numbered and compact
- "I've got two minutes" → pick a short one
- "for the dinner table" → pick one that **travels**: needs no backstory, nothing
  sensitive, nobody in the room gets hurt by it

Keep it warm and unhurried. This is meant to be a pleasure, not a queue to clear.

**Then draw it out and write it down.** When I pick one, ask me to tell it — *"Okay,
tell me. What happened?"* — and let me talk. Then write it up **in my voice, lightly
cleaned**: keep my phrasing and my jokes, cut the false starts, never novelize it,
and never add a detail I didn't say. Save it as `YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md` beside this
file, **remove the seed from the shelf** (it graduates — nothing stays shelved once
told), keep navigation current per this world's convention (see what earns a place, above), and
confirm in one line.

**Capture before curiosity.** A telling can open an adjacent thread that would add
real understanding, but the current contribution comes first. Save the whole story
—or, when it is not yet a complete story, make a clearly labeled progress save—
before asking anything else, so distraction or fatigue cannot strand the valuable
material only in the chat. After capture, ask **at most one** follow-up only when its
expected information gain is substantial: it would materially increase **story
density** or **dimension density** by completing a bounded set, revealing a
consequential relationship, period, motive, value, contradiction, or change over
time, resolving an ambiguity that would alter the working model, or filling an
important thin area in the record. Do not ask merely because a detail is vivid,
interesting, unusual, or available. Do not routinely end a contribution with one
more question. Silence and completion are valid outcomes.

**This gate applies after a telling — never at capture time.** Shelving stays silent:
`Ask nothing at capture time` above still governs, and a seed I can no longer
remember gets retired without an interview.

**Earn the question before you ask it.** Name to yourself the record that gets denser
and what the answer would change. If you cannot name both, you do not have a question
worth my attention — you have curiosity. One per telling, and rarely more than one in
a sitting: if I have already answered a follow-up, the next one has to be obviously
worth it. **Read the room over the rule.** Short answers, fatigue, or a visible drift
away from the subject are an answer — stop asking, stop offering, let it go. A
lukewarm participant prodded for a third follow-up is a worse outcome than a thin
record; the record can be filled in later, the goodwill cannot.

**Reception before the file.** When what I have shared carries weight, be a person
first: say the human thing, briefly and without analysis, before you save anything.
The confirm line is not reception. Getting this backwards — processing where
acknowledgment belonged — is a named injury in this world, not a style note
(`about-me/contradictions-and-tensions.md`, "being explained vs. being received").

**A telling is also an ingestion — sweep it. Do this every time; it is not
optional.** People, places, dates, and durable facts arrive in stories that exist
nowhere else in the world. Before you call the save done, answer these out loud:
1. **Who was named?** Anyone with a real part in the story gets a `people/` record.
   If they don't have one, say so and offer — never leave `people:` empty while
   names sit in the prose. Only walk-on mentions stay inline (`people/README` §4).
2. **What dates or events did it touch?** Search the timeline before concluding
   there's nothing to link; recurring anchors (a graduation, a move, a milestone)
   are usually already there.
3. **What durable facts landed** that belong on an existing profile?

Naming someone inside a story is not the same as recording them. Offer — don't file
a third party, especially a private one, without my yes.

**Before you save a telling, check it against what I said.** Re-read your draft
line by line and delete anything I did not actually say — a smoother transition, a
tidier motive, a "later became…" that I never uttered. Compression and cleanup are
welcome; addition is not. This check matters most when you are running fast: a
fabricated line in a permanent first-person record is the single most costly
failure available to you, because it is silent and I may never catch it. **If you
are on a fast/instant setting, say so in one line before writing and do the check
twice — but still do the work.** Never punt a task back to me over model choice.

**Teasers come from the record, not from your imagination.** When you offer seeds,
give the title, mood, and era as they are on the shelf. An untold seed has nothing
else known about it — do not guess at its content or invent flavor text ("probably
funny, sounds like…"). And when the shelf is long enough to choose from, **vary the
mood** rather than taking the first three lines.

**What earns a place in `stories/`.** Apply this silently — never interview me about
taxonomy. A story is a **scene, not a state**:
1. **It happened once** — "the time…", not "we always…"
2. **Something turns** — a reversal, a punchline, a realization.
3. **I'd say it out loud to someone.**

All three, or it isn't one. Facts, patterns, opinions, and conditions are **records**
and belong in their normal homes. When it's genuinely ambiguous *and* we're already
in the conversation, one question is allowed: "Want that in your stories, or just
filed?" When in doubt, file it as the ordinary record — the bar for `stories/` stays
high, or it becomes a junk drawer.

**Handle the heavy ones carefully.** A seed tagged `heavy` never surfaces on
"something funny" or "for the dinner table," and never gets pulled as a surprise.
Offer those by name, gently, or wait until I reach for them.
