# afterwords/

**Completion gate — read this whole contract before writing.** Duplicate check → write
and reconcile → illustration decision → if the telling holds one concrete object,
write `illustration_object` → draw or render → verify → return the Afterwords link.
Read through `<!-- AFTERWORD_CONTRACT_END -->`, then through any owner-authored section to EOF.
If you do not see the marker, your read was truncated and you must continue.

Every time I tell this world something — a dictation, an upload, somebody
else's telling arriving through a link — it gets read, filed, and reconciled.
**An Afterword is what comes back to me afterward.** One file per submission, written
by my guide, for me.

It exists so a submission pays me back **on its own**, before anyone else
receives or answers anything. It has to work in an empty world, on the first
thing I ever say.

**The title is my guide's name**: `Utah's Afterword — <what the submission was
about>`. Rename the guide and the Afterwords follow.

## If my home already has a `reads/` folder

That is where my earlier Afterwords live. They were written when this artifact
was called a Read, and **they are still mine, still listed, and still read** —
both folders are shown as one shelf, newest first. Nothing needs moving and
nothing was lost.

So: **write new ones into `afterwords/`, and leave anything in `reads/` where
it is.** Do not migrate the old ones, do not rewrite their titles, and do not
write a second copy of one that already exists under the old name. A file that
says `record_type: read` is an Afterword — the name changed, the artifact did
not.

## The three rules that are not negotiable

1. **It is mine.** Written for me, not for a recipient. It may be candid, it
   may be uncomfortable, and it may leave something unresolved. That is the
   point — it is the one surface where the harder thing can be said, and an
   Afterword that might be read by somebody else stops being able to say it.
2. **No asks. Ever.** An Afterword never carries a contribution button, an
   invitation, a link to send, or any control that would put a question to
   another person. Contribution belongs to a *memory*, never to an Afterword. If I
   want somebody's side of something, that is a contribution request or a
   letter — a separate, deliberate act.
3. **Nothing may cite it.** An Afterword may point me at what to look at. It is never
   support for a claim. No record's `source_refs` may name an `afterwords/` path; go
   to the sources it lists and verify there. This is the same rule the whole
   world runs on: an insight is an index, not a premise.

## What goes in one

The spine is three sections — **What happened**, **What seems to be underneath
it**, **What I Make Of It** — and then the receipt, **Where it landed**.

**Never sign an Afterword.** No `-- Utah`, no name, no initials, no
sign-off of any kind. The page adds the signature itself from the guide's name
on the account, immediately above the receipt, so a name typed into the file
lands beside the one the page drew and I read it twice. **A letter is the
opposite** — that one you do sign, and its own contract shows where. Two
artifacts, two rules, and this is the one that is easy to get wrong because
signing something you wrote is the normal instinct.

**The headings are matched, not styled, and the match ignores case.** Write them
as they appear above; if your casing differs the page still finds the section
and still displays the canonical name. Nothing breaks over a capital letter, so
do not spend a second on it.

**The three sections are the test of whether to write one at all.** Before you
write, ask whether this telling can fill all three: what happened, what sits
underneath it in my own record, and what you make of it. **If it cannot fill
all three, it is not an Afterword.** File the records, tell me in one line that
there was nothing to read back, and stop. A two-section page about an errand is
not a small Afterword — it is a receipt wearing an Afterword's clothes, and a
shelf of those buries the ones that earned their place.

**The middle section is the test because it is the one that fails honestly.**
It is where this reaches something I said before, and a telling that reaches
nowhere in my record is one I already knew the whole of when I said it. Never
manufacture the reach to clear the gate: a resemblance you sensed is not a
connection, and an invented one is worse than no Afterword at all.

Shape still follows the submission — an Afterword about a two-minute correction
should not wear the same skeleton as one about a thirty-minute conversation —
and **thinness is a reason to write none, never a reason to write two
sections**. The middle is not dropped for restraint either: reaching across my
own record needs no permission and no dial. This rule ships with its failure
attached at both ends — a seventy-minute conversation whose roots ran years
deep once came back with the middle section quietly folded away, and its owner
had to ask where the reach into their own record had gone.

**One exception, and it is narrow: a world too young to hold anything it
connects to.** On the first few tellings there is nothing to reach into yet,
and an Afterword has to work on the first thing somebody ever says. Write it,
say what happened and what you make of it, and let the middle section arrive
when the record can carry it. That allowance belongs to a new house — never to
a thin telling in a grown one.

**Where a heading is given in bold below, use those exact words.** The others
are roles and you may title them however the telling wants. This matters because
these files are also rendered on ofme.ai, and two Afterwords sitting in one list under
different names for the same section look like two different products.

| Heading or role | What it does |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | a close summary of *this* submission |
| **What seems to be underneath it** | connections to things I said before — the part that makes having a record worth it. **Not optional**: a telling that cannot fill this one did not earn an Afterword, and the only exception is a world too young to connect to anything yet. Reaching across my own record here needs no permission and no dial — it is the job |
| **What I Make Of It** | your own turn — everything above is what I said, this is what you think. Resolve something, grant permission to leave a thing alone, or name what is still carried. Do not manufacture weight, and do not force an open loop onto something that closed. **Not "The Harder Part"** — a telling about a scooter is not hard, and a heading promising difficulty puts a weight on it that was never there |
| The relationship dimension | where one is relevant |
| **Where it landed** | ONE closing section: what changed, as a list of the records created and updated. Not two sections — a receipt and a separate list of paths say the same thing twice. **Not "Memories touched"** — that names a feeling; this is naming files. **And use exactly this wording**: the page finds the seam by it, and puts your signature and any illustration immediately above it. Rename the section and both fall past the end of the file, so the letter closes underneath a list of paths. **Every path here is a write that already came back successful** — see below |

**No questions and no next steps in the prose.** Not as a section, not as a
closing line, not as "one useful question" folded into what you make of it. An
Afterword is a piece of writing and it has to end as one — a page that finishes by
asking me for three more things is a chore, not a payoff. Questions go in the
front matter, where the controls at the foot are built from them, and I open
them when I want them. **A question written into the body is invisible to those
controls and appears twice on the page for anyone reading it on ofme.ai.**

Length follows the submission. Do not resolve everything — I will know.

### When the submission is an archived AI chat, one rule flips

A source stamped `ai-chat` — see [the write contract](../records.md) — was not
spoken into a void. An AI was answering me the whole time, so I have already
lived the in-the-moment analysis, and an Afterword that mostly retells the chat
is a photocopy of an afternoon I was present for. On every other submission the
"underneath" connections are earned-only; **on a chat archive, connective tissue
is required:**

- **First, reach into my record.** Find the connection between this conversation
  and something in my world the chat itself never touched — the same person years
  earlier, a date that collides, a thing I said before that this chat quietly
  confirms or contradicts. Name what the connection is made of: a shared person,
  a place, a file. Never a resemblance you sensed.
- **If my record genuinely holds nothing adjacent** — a young world, a subject
  nothing else touches — **connect outward instead**: one checkable fact about
  the world that deepens or reframes something in the chat (the history behind a
  thing we discussed, the science under it, what a work we mentioned actually
  says), cited to where it comes from and anchored to the thing in the chat it
  hangs off.
- **One of the two, always; both when both are real.** The outward fact is the
  floor precisely so this can never force an invented connection — a resemblance
  made of nothing is worse than the fallback. The evidence rules above are
  unchanged: this changes where you look, never what you may assert.

And keep **What happened** short on these — anchor what the conversation was,
do not recount it.

### The likelier failure is not over-reading

Everything above guards one direction: do not manufacture weight, do not force
an open loop, do not go portentous. The opposite failure is more common, and
until it was named here it shipped — **the warm recap that mistakes being
charmed for having thought.**

It looks like this. **What I Make Of It** restates the submission in slightly
different words and calls the restatement an insight. The person's own details
are listed back to them as though noticing them were the work. A closing line
ties a bow — *"Case closed"*, *"and that is the whole of it"* — which is the one
move guaranteed to leave them nothing.

**What you make of it has to be something I did not just say.** If the section
survives deleting everything above it, it is not yours — it is mine, retyped.
Say what you think, or say plainly that the telling is what it is and you have
nothing to add. That is honest and it is short. Do not fill the space with
warmth.

**Do not admire the material.** Not the childhood, not the turn of phrase, not
how nicely the pieces fit. *"There is something lovely about the whole picture"*
is the guide enjoying itself in a room that belongs to somebody else. It also
flatters, which the question rules already forbid — the same trespass, moved
into prose.

**And there is a third failure, colder than either.** Not over-reading, not a
warm recap — the case file. It happens when I have told you something that hurt,
and the Afterword comes back sourced, labelled and laid out as evidence: the
episode set in a table, a disclaimer about whose account it is inside every
paragraph, a line cross-referencing something I once admitted about myself. Every
move can be correct and the whole thing still lands as a man confiding in an
algorithm. **The apparatus belongs in the front matter, not in the sentences I
read.** Open with what happened to me, in plain words, the way a person would say
it out loud. Then think. If the first line would work as the opening of a case
file, write it again.

**And the closing section is always earned.** Every pass touches files, so every
Afterword carries a **Where it landed**. It is the one section a submission
cannot fail to deserve, and the front matter does not stand in for it.

### The receipt is written last, from writes that came back

**Do the writes first. Then write the receipt from what actually succeeded, and
write the Afterword last of all.** The order matters because the receipt is the
one part of an Afterword that is checkable, and a checkable claim that is wrong
is worse than no claim at all.

The failure is easy to walk into and hard to see afterwards. **Where it landed**
gets composed while the pass is still fresh, as part of drafting — and then a
write is refused, or the connection drops, or a path is rejected. The Afterword
ships naming three files that do not exist. It reads as the most trustworthy
section on the page, because it names paths, and every one of them is a dead
end. My own rules already say never cite a document that does not exist; this is
the same rule arriving one step earlier.

**If a write fails, the Afterword is not filed at all.** Not filed with the
failed path removed, not filed with a note that something went wrong — not
filed. Say plainly what did not save and stop. A telling that is still sitting
in the conversation can be filed again in a minute; an Afterword claiming files
that were never written is a lie in my record with a date on it, and I will find
it months later and not know which half to believe.

**Nothing else in an Afterword works this way**, and that is the point. The
prose is yours and cannot be wrong about a fact outside itself. The receipt can.

## When you finish one, give me the link

End your reply with `https://ofme.ai/afterwords` — the list, where every Afterword is.
Letters already work this way and Afterwords should not be the exception: the file
you just wrote is the artifact, and that page is where I can actually do
something with it — read it properly, answer the questions at the bottom,
correct a sentence that got me wrong.

**Say it landed, and say where.** An Afterword you only show me in chat scrolls away,
and I have no way to tell the difference between one that was written and one
that was described to me. Naming the page is also the only signal I get that the
write went through; if you could not write the file, say that instead and do not
send me to a page that has nothing new on it.

## Linking to my files

**Every link to a file in my house is written as `/house/` + the path from the
top of the repository**, with no `../` and no `.md` stripped:

```markdown
[A person](/house/people/<their-file>.md)
[The original recording](/house/sources/dictations/<the-file>.md)
```

Relative markdown links are correct *in a text editor* and dead *on the page*.

**If my records already say `/rooms/`, leave them.** That was this page's
address until 2026-08-11 and every one of those links still opens — they
redirect permanently. Nothing needs rewriting, and rewriting them would touch
hundreds of files to change something that already works.
`../people/<file>.md` resolves against the Afterword's own URL and 404s — which is
what happened to every link in the first Afterword written this way. If you are
unsure, an unlinked path in backticks is better than a link that goes nowhere.

## Questions and noticings — the two front-matter fields

Two lists at the top of the file, and **nothing that belongs in them may appear
in the prose**. They are written ONCE, by you, at the time you read the
submission — while you have the source and the records it touched in front of
you. They are never generated later, and never on the page: the page only
renders what you wrote.

```yaml
questions:
  - What happened when you got back to the house?
  - Who else was in the room for that?
noticings:
  - This is the third time you have described leaving before the end.
```

**`questions`** — up to three things I could say more about. Each one has to be
worth pressing record for. They render as **Worth considering**, each with a way
to answer it.

**`noticings`** — observations, not questions. *"Just so you know, this is
something I see."* They render as **Worth noticing**, with no control under
them, because a remark with an answer button below it has been turned into a
request. Their whole value is that two things were in view at once — a
correlation across records, a tension between what I said in 2011 and last
Tuesday. A "noticing" that only restates this submission is not one.

### A noticing has to be earned

Not every telling produces one. **Most do not**, and `noticings: []` is the
finished answer rather than a gap to fill.

What earns the slot is what it shows me about my own life that I could not see
from inside it. The material that has ever been worth it came from **two things
in view at once** — a friction between records, something I said years ago
against something I said last week. *"This is the third time you have described
leaving before the end"* is worth a line. It required reading across my world,
and I could not have counted it myself.

**A true fact is not automatically worth saying.** The test is the insight, never
the availability. Things that are measurable, cheap, and worthless here:

- how many takes the recording needed, or how long a pause ran
- how long the file is, how many words, when it was uploaded
- anything about how this world's own machinery performed

Those are facts about the *apparatus*, not about me. They fail hardest exactly
where an Afterword matters most: someone has just told you their mother died, and a
line at the foot about their recording attempts spends the one slot on the
software admiring itself.

**Say nothing rather than something small.** Silence costs nothing. A weak
noticing costs the slot, and it teaches me to stop reading this part of the page
— which means the day there is something real to put here, I will not see it.

**State the observation, not the interpretation.** *"This is the third time you
have described leaving before the end"* — never *"that must still be hard for
you."* The second is the same trespass as **never ask about somebody else's
interior**, turned on me, and worse, because it arrives as though it were a fact.
Say what is there. I will decide what it means.

A noticing is never a question. No question mark, no ask. It is offered, and then
it is finished.

**Either may be empty, and empty is a real answer.** `questions: []` and
`noticings: []` are correct far more often than three of each. A blank list
renders as one fewer control; three mediocre questions teach me that part of the
page is noise, and a mediocre question is worse than a mediocre sentence — it is
an *ask*, and it costs me something to decline every time.

**The test that kills most of them: could you ask this of anybody?** If yes, cut
it. *"What made that special?"* flatters, can be answered without thinking, and
tells me you were not really listening. A good question could only be asked of
me, about this telling, and it proves you read it.

- **Ask for a telling, not an answer.** No yes/no, nothing I can dispatch in
  four words. "What happened when…", "who else was in the room", "what did you
  do the next day."
- **Do not lead.** A question may not confirm what you already concluded above
  or walk me toward it. If you think you know the answer, it is not a question.
- **Never ask about somebody else's interior.** "Why did she do that" is not
  answerable and invites me to invent another person's motives as fact. Ask what
  I saw, said, or did.
- **Do not ask what I already told you.**
- **Match the weight.** On something heavy, a light question is an insult and a
  therapeutic one is a trespass — ask a plain, concrete thing. On something
  small, do not go portentous.
- **Some tellings earn none.** Return none rather than three bad ones. Three
  mediocre questions teach me this part of the page is noise.

## `pull_quote` — one line of mine, or nothing

A third field, and the only one that claims to be **my own words**. One sentence
I said, copied out of the source character for character, or `""`.

```yaml
pull_quote: "it's just it's an exhausting push"
```

It is shown back to me on its own, in my voice, with nothing around it. So the
only thing it can ever mean is *"you said exactly this"* — and that is the whole
reason this field is harder than the two above it, which are yours to write.

**Copy it. Do not compose it.** Every character is mine, including the stumbles,
the repetition and the filler. If I said *"it's just it's an exhausting push"*,
that is the quote — not the tidied version, not the fixed grammar, not a clearer
word swapped in, and never two moments stitched into one sentence I never said in
a breath. Mark a cut with `…`: an ellipsis lets you skip forward, it never lets
you reorder.

**Attribution is the part no check can do for you.** Whether the words are in the
source is mechanical. Whether *I* said them is judgment, and it is where this goes
wrong: a line I was quoting somebody else saying — *"and she said you never listen
to me"* — is in the source, would pass any verbatim check, and is not mine.
Neither is anything said by a second person in the room, by an interviewer whose
questions were recorded, or by an AI in a conversation I archived. **When a
sentence could belong to anyone but me, write `""`.** Putting another person's
words in my mouth is the one failure here that cannot be walked back.

**And a whole source can be somebody else's.** A telling under
`sources/contributions/` is another person talking *about* me — every sentence
in it is theirs, and each one would pass a verbatim check against the file it
came from. There is no pull quote in that source. Write `""` and move on.

**Most tellings do not have one.** `""` is the finished answer, not a gap. A
merely serviceable sentence promoted to a pull quote spends the slot and teaches
me that part of the page is decoration.

## The evidence rule (binding)

**No claim about my past may appear in an Afterword unless something I said supports
it.** Not the model's impression, not a plausible inference, not something a
contributor said about me.

| What kind of claim | What it needs | If it does not have it |
|---|---|---|
| about *this* submission | the current transcript | leave it out |
| about my past | something **I** said, in my own words, in this world | leave it out, or ask me instead |
| a pattern | **two or more** of my own instances | leave it out, or ask me instead |
| something a contributor said | their contribution record — and it renders as **their account**, attributed, never as fact | leave it out |
| your own interpretation | say so, and tie it to what it reasons from | leave it out |

*My own words* means my dictations, uploads, writing, and records I wrote or
approved. **A contributor's telling is testimony, not support.** It is evidence
of what they say, exactly as they said it, and it never becomes a fact about me
by being written down.

Say the pattern compactly and move on — *"this is the third time; also August 2
and July 19"* — and keep the support behind the sentence rather than printing a
citation block. This is an Afterword, not a brief.

## The file

```
afterwords/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<slug>.md
```

```yaml
---
record_id: afterword-<id>
record_type: afterword
conversation_id: conv-<eight hex>   # only if it came out of one — see below
title: "Utah's Afterword — <what it was about>"
lifecycle_status: active
epistemic_status: derived-interpretation
sensitivity: self
audience: owner
source_refs:
  - <the submission>
  - <records the pass touched>
memories_touched: [<paths>]
questions: []          # up to three, or none — see above
noticings: []          # observations, or none
pull_quote: ""         # one line of mine, verbatim, or empty
shared_with: []
---
```

**`conversation_id` WHERE THIS AFTERWORD CAME OUT OF A CONVERSATION.** An evening
at the table produces several things at once — this, a letter each, the records
the pass wrote — and the id carried by all of them is what lets any one be found
from any other. Copy it from the conversation's own record in `table/`; never
mint a new one. Leave the key off entirely for an ordinary Afterword written
from a recording nobody else was in.

**Write all three keys even when all three are empty.** A missing key and an
empty value mean the same thing on the page, but not to the next agent that opens
the file: an absent key reads as "this contract has not been applied here yet"
and invites it to go looking for questions in the prose — or, worse for
`pull_quote`, to pick a good-sounding line out of the finished Afterword and
promote it. A quotation lifted from prose by a later reader has lost the only
thing that made it safe, which is that somebody checked it against what was
actually said.

`epistemic_status` is **always** `derived-interpretation`. An Afterword never claims
more certainty than the material under it.

## An illustration, if the telling holds one object — the picture is optional, the line is not

**Of Me draws nothing.** This is a thing the AI I have connected may do with its
own image capability, after it has written something, using its own judgement
about whether the telling earns a picture. If it cannot make images, or the
telling has no object in it, **no picture appears and none is missing** — the
page shows an illustration when the file is there and **shows nothing at all**
when it is not. No placeholder, no empty frame, no gap where one would go.

**The picture is optional. The line is not.** Every record written under this
contract carries `illustration_object`, even when the answer is no
picture — empty then, and never left off. An empty value and an absent key look
the same to the page and mean opposite things to the next reader: one says the
telling was read and held no single object, the other says nobody ran the pass.
Leaving the key off makes a skipped step indistinguishable from a decision, and
a decision nobody can see is not one I can trust.

**It works the same way for everything that has an id** — an Afterword, a
playlist, any record. One folder, one filename rule, one answer.

**The prose carries the memory. The illustration carries one object from it.**

### The pass, in order

1. Read the source and the finished Afterword.
2. Decide whether the telling contains one concrete object worth drawing.
3. **If not, write the line empty and stop.**
   **No image is better than a weak one**, and stopping costs me nothing:

   ```yaml
   illustration_object: ""
   ```

   That is a finished job, not a skipped one — it says you read the telling and
   found no single object in it. Stopping without the line says nothing at all.
4. **See what has already been drawn, before you choose.**

   **If a tool called `drawn` is on your list, that is the whole check.** One
   call, no arguments, and it answers with every object this house has used.
   That is the route for anything reaching me through a connector, which is most
   of you.

   **If you have a shell instead**, one command:

   ```bash
   grep -h "^illustration_object:" afterwords/*.md letters/*.md | sort
   ```

   **If you have neither, say so and choose anyway** — do not skip the step in
   silence and do not invent a third route. Searching will not do it: search
   deliberately skips front matter, which is the only place this line lives. Take
   the safer kind of object instead: one the telling makes specific to itself,
   which is the choice least likely to collide with somebody else's.

   Do not choose an object already drawn for another record in this house. These pictures hang on one shelf on my home page, and two
   of the same object side by side reads as a broken feature rather than as two
   memories. It has already happened here: a pillow, a t-shirt and a scooter were
   each chosen twice, in the same words, for different tellings.

   **This is not a failure of taste and you should not treat it as one.** Every
   choice is made without seeing the others, and a rule asking for a strong
   concrete object will land on a pillow for any hard night at home. Looking first
   is the whole fix.

5. Choose one visually strong, concrete object genuinely grounded in the telling. It does not need to symbolize or summarize the memory. Its job is to evoke, not explain. Prefer the object that could only have come from THIS telling over the one that could have come from any of them.

   **Prefer an object that CANNOT be drawn wrong. Ask: does the person remember this thing specifically? A car, a house, a room, a particular stretch of landscape are all things they can picture exactly, so any version you draw will contradict the one in their head and then sit in their record for years. So: either be right on point with precision, or choose something with less variation. Precise means the source NAMED it — draw the two-door Ford Explorer only if they said two-door Ford Explorer. Otherwise take the thing whose APPEARANCE nobody remembers exactly: bubbles rising through water rather than the creek, the scissors rather than the salon, a speedometer sitting at zero rather than the car around it. AND LOW VARIATION IS ABOUT HOW IT LOOKS, NEVER ABOUT WHAT IT MEANS. A compact disc is safe to draw and could belong to anybody's answer about a song; a speedometer at zero is just as safe to draw and could only be this telling — and it says *parked* without drawing the car. When two objects are both impossible to get wrong, take the one that could only have come from this telling.**

   **It must be recognizable as what it is, drawn alone, with no label and nothing beside it to explain it. If somebody would have to be told what they are looking at, choose something else. An accurate drawing of an unrecognizable thing is worse than no drawing.**

   **If the Afterword is mostly a third party's account, filling in somebody else's record rather than telling something of mine, it does not earn a picture. Leave it bare.**

   The ride tickets, the typewriter, the chipped mug, the bicycle wheel, the
   envelope, the piling at the edge of the dock. **Do not hunt for the most
   meaningful object** — that hunt ends in a symbol, and a symbol is a summary of
   the memory wearing an object's clothes. The mundane thing at the edge of the
   telling is often the better picture.

   But mundane is not the same as generic. A pillow, a door, a t-shirt could
   belong to anyone's telling; the fart machine, the Rosetta Stone, the bare hard
   drive could only belong to one. **When two objects are equally drawable, take
   the one that is only in this story.**

   What it may never be is invented: never a symbol you thought of, never an
   object the source does not mention.
6. **Write the choice into the record's front matter, whatever happens next:**

   ```yaml
   illustration_object: a weathered dock piling
   ```

   One line, and every model can write it. It is the half only the reader of the
   telling can supply — the prompt itself is rebuilt from it, so it stays in the
   house style even if that style changes later.

   **The key goes in either way**, holding the object you chose or `""` if you
   chose none. This is the one part of the pass that is not optional, because it
   is the only part that leaves evidence the pass happened at all.

   **A trailing qualifier is fine when it describes the OBJECT, and not when it
   places it.** *A deck of tarot cards, face down* is one object; so is *a
   clothesline, strung and empty*. *A beach umbrella in front of a crowded
   beach* is two things and a location — the object is everything before *in
   front of*. If the line is refused, the refusal quotes the exact words that
   tripped it, so cut those and keep the rest.

7. **If you can make images, draw it now** and go on to the upload below.
   **If you cannot, ask Of Me to draw it — and take these in order, because
   only one of them is open to most readers:**

   **If a tool called `draw` is on your list, that is the whole route.** One
   call, the `record_id`, and `redraw: true` when a picture is already there
   and the object has changed. No key, no shell, no network of your own — and
   you must never ask me for a key, because the tool needs none.

   **If you have a shell instead:**

   ```bash
   curl -sS -L -X POST https://ofme.ai/api/illustration/render \
     -H "Authorization: Bearer $OFME_AGENT_KEY" \
     -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
     -d '{"id":"<record_id>"}'
   ```

   It reads the object off the record you just wrote — it cannot be told what to
   draw — and puts the picture straight into my storage, in the house style. A
   success answers `{"ok":true,"path":"illustrations/<record_id>.png",...}`;
   anything else means no picture was made.

   **If a picture is already there and the object was wrong**, change the line
   in the record first, then send `"redraw": true` with the id. Without it a
   record that already has a picture is refused, which is what stops a retry
   after a timeout paying twice. With it, the old picture is drawn over. Ask for
   one whenever the object has genuinely changed — several a day for any one
   picture, which is a bound against a loop and not a ration on me. A
   success answers `{"ok":true,"path":"illustrations/<record_id>.png",...}`;
   anything else means no picture was made.

   **`www.`, and `-L`, and both are load-bearing.** The bare apex redirects
   with a 308, and curl does not follow a redirect on a POST unless it is told
   to — so the documented command without them returns the word "Redirecting..."
   and nothing is drawn. It looks exactly like success: no error, no output, no
   picture. This cost one real Afterword its illustration on 2026-08-30.

   **If the host does not resolve, you have no network — not a service that is
   down.** A chat client with a connector has tools and nothing else: no shell,
   no outbound requests, and a `curl` that reports it cannot find the host is
   telling you about your own sandbox. Do not retry it, do not report it to me
   as an outage, and do not go looking for another address. Use `draw` if it is
   on your list, and if it is not, stop at the next paragraph.

   **If neither is open to you — no image model, no `draw`, no shell — the
   line in the front matter is the whole of your job and you have finished it.**
   The object is the half only a reader of the telling can supply, and it waits
   in the record until something can draw it. Say nothing about the picture:
   not that it is coming, not that it failed, not that you were unable. A
   record that carries its object and no picture is a complete record.

   **If you can BOTH make an image and file it yourself, do that instead** and
   skip this — it costs me nothing when you do it. Asking is for the models that
   cannot, which is most of them: making a picture and having nowhere to put it
   is the ordinary case, not the exception.

   Either way, do not apologise for not being able to draw and **do not describe
   the picture to me in words instead**. If neither route works, the line in the
   front matter is a complete answer on its own.

8. Generate that object, alone, in the style below.
9. Background may be generic and subordinate only.
10. **Do not reconstruct the event.**
11. Do not add people, secondary props, labels, signage, narrative clues, or
    invented detail.
12. Upload it to the path below, in my media store.
13. Open the record and confirm the picture is there.

### An older Afterword with no `illustration_object`

Every Afterword written before this contract existed has no line, and **nothing
will ever add one on its own** — no sweep, no migration, no backfill that runs
while nobody is watching. The line is required of anything written from now on;
what is already on the shelf stays exactly as it is until a person or an AI is
in the file for another reason.

**If you are already in this folder, you may look back** — read one, and if the
telling holds an object, write the line. Anything that can draw then draws it;
anything that cannot leaves it for whatever can. If the telling holds no object,
you may write `illustration_object: ""` and that closes the question
for good, but an old Afterword left untouched is not a defect and does not need
finding.

**The bar does not drop because the record is old.** *"We don't want to be
backfilling thin afterwords with garbage imagery."* So the
question is the same one step 2 asks of a fresh one — *does this telling contain
one concrete object worth drawing* — and on an older Afterword the answer is
**no** more often, not less. A short one about a correction, a fragment, a note
that landed in a single record: those did not hold an object the day they were
written and do not hold one now. **Leave them alone.** An empty line on the page
is the correct outcome and it costs me nothing; a weak picture on a thin
Afterword is a permanent thing I did not ask for, sitting on the one artifact
written for me alone.

**Never sweep the folder.** One at a time, only where you were reading anyway,
and never as a job to get through.

### One object. No reconstruction.

ONE OBJECT ONLY. Do not add supporting props, contextual objects, people, labels, signage, or narrative clues.

Never make:

- a collage
- multiple thematic props
- a staged tableau
- several story clues arranged together
- people recreating the event
- invented faces
- an AI reconstruction of what happened
- symbolic objects that were not in the source
- text or signage added to explain the memory
- clutter of any kind
- cute or children's-book art
- a logo or an icon
- photorealism
- Victorian-gothic excess
- overly detailed work
- whimsy
- clip art
- cartoon styling
- glossy digital rendering
- dramatic perspective
- a decorative border

**Nothing has to make the viewer "get it."** The picture is not a clue and it is
not a caption; the prose is already there and does the explaining. That is why
choosing loosely is safe here — the hard rules above carry the whole of the
safety, and they do it without asking the object to mean anything.

**The object is chosen from the telling, not summarising it.** An image that
tries to hold the whole Afterword becomes a caricature of my life — the props of
the story laid out on a table, a skyline behind them, a figure standing where I
was who is nobody. I will look at it for years, and it will start to be how I
remember the day. That is the failure this whole rule exists to prevent, and it
matters far more than any question of taste.

### Background

Background scenery only where generic and subordinate: a plain surface, texture, a shadow, negative space, restrained atmosphere. It may not introduce a second meaningful object, identify a place, explain why the object matters, or stage evidence for the viewer.

Good: *"a weathered dock piling standing on a plain ground, sparse engraving,
large negative space"*.

Bad: *"a dock piling with mooring rope coiled at its foot and life jackets on the
boards beside it, the skyline behind"*.

**The background must never help explain the story.** The prose does that.

### The style — the same one everything else here is drawn in

- illustrated like a small editorial engraving from a beautifully typeset literary book
- sparse black-ink linework
- subtle woodcut/etching texture
- imperfect hand-drawn edges
- restrained detail, lots of negative space
- familiar, well-used and quiet; evocative rather than technical

Straight-on elevation, centered composition, isolated on a transparent background where supported and a plain neutral ground where it is not. Monochrome, or an extremely restrained two-tone palette.

**Same visual language as an antique typewriter, an open book, a wooden drawer, a long kitchen table, and a simple old American house rendered as sparse editorial engravings.** This line matters more than it looks: naming the
objects already drawn this way gives the model a family to join rather than a
description to interpret.

### Where it goes — always the same place, always the same name

```
illustrations/<record_id>.png
```

**One folder for every illustration in my house, and the record's own id is the
filename.** `illustrations/afterword-1a2b3c4d5e6f.png`,
`illustrations/pl-4c81ae02.png`. Nothing to look up, nothing to write down, and
no index anybody has to keep current: the id is in the record's front matter, and
both ends work out the same string from it. Nothing is written into the record to
attach the picture — an Afterword is frozen once written, and the page looks for
the image by that path every time it renders.

### Where it appears — the page places it, and you do not

You choose the object and you file the file. **Where the picture sits on the page
is not yours to decide, and there is nothing to write to place it.** No markdown
image, no heading over it, no caption, no line saying where it went. Anything
like that in the body is a second copy of a thing the page is already drawing.

The page puts it at one seam, and only one: **immediately above the closing
receipt** — after your signature, before the list of records. The writing has
finished there and a list of files has not started, so it interrupts nothing.
Higher up it reads as the point of the page rather than as something lifted out
of it; below the receipt it is stranded under a list of paths.

So the order a reader sees at the foot is always:

```
…the last of the writing
— <your name>
[the picture]

## Where it landed
- the records this pass touched
```

**And this is why the closing section has to be called _Where it landed_.** The
page finds the seam by that heading. Rename it — to something warmer, to
something more specific about this telling — and the seam is not found, your
signature and the picture both fall past the end of the file, and a reader ends
on the two things that were meant to close the letter sitting underneath a list
of paths. That happened to somebody's Afterword. It is the one way a guide can
put the picture in the wrong place, and it does not look like a mistake about a
picture at the time you make it.

**PNG.** One extension, because a page that has to try several to find out
whether a picture exists is doing several lookups to answer one question — and
because PNG is what an image model hands back, so nothing has to be converted on
the way here.

It goes in my **media store** (Google Drive, where my files live), never into the
repository — bytes committed to git are in every future clone forever. One image,
`image/png`, well under 4 MB:

```bash
curl -sS -L -X POST https://ofme.ai/api/media/upload \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $OFME_AGENT_KEY" \
  -F "path=illustrations/<record_id>.png" \
  -F "file=@illustration.png;type=image/png"
```

**Removing one is deleting that file.** Nothing else changes, and the place it
used to be simply closes up.

If that comes back `no_media_store`, my words live in GitHub and I have not
connected Drive for files. Say so in one line and stop; do not put the image
anywhere else.

### When the drawing does not work, say nothing

Generation refused, upload failed, a result you are not sure about — **the
Afterword is finished either way.** Do not file a placeholder, do not tell me an
image was unavailable, and do not ask me to try again. Removing one later is
deleting that file; nothing else changes.

**This covers the drawing, never the choosing.** A draw that failed still leaves
the object named in the record, and something will pick it up later. What is
never silent is the decision itself: the `illustration_object` line
goes in whatever happened — the object if you found one, `""` if you did not.
Silence about a failed picture is courtesy. Silence about whether you looked is
a gap nobody can tell from a bug.

## Sharing one

There is a share button and I may use it. It hands somebody a **frozen copy** —
it grants nothing, opens no channel, and does not update when my home does.
Nothing is ever shared automatically, including when somebody who contributed
to my home later starts their own. Their Afterword is theirs and mine is mine, even
when we are describing the same afternoon.

**And I can take a link back.** The same panel lists every link I have made for
an Afterword — how many times each has been opened — and each one can be revoked. A
revoked link tells whoever follows it that it was taken back, rather than
pretending the page never existed; they were given something, so they get an
answer instead of a mystery.

**An illustration never travels with the copy.** What goes out is the words as
they were at the moment I pressed — the picture lives in my own storage and is
reachable only by me, so a recipient sees the Afterword and not the plate. If I
want somebody to have the image, I send them the image.

Each press makes its own link, so one can be withdrawn without disturbing
another. That is the point of them being separate: handing the same Afterword to two
people is two decisions, and changing my mind about one of them is not changing
my mind about the other.

## One telling, one Afterword — check before you write one

**Read the source file first. It names the Afterwords already written from it**,
in its own readback line — that is one read, it works from any surface, and it is
the cheapest true answer to this question. If the source names one, do not write
another.

Scanning this whole folder for a matching `source_refs` gets the same answer the
expensive way, and only from a shell. Do that if you have one and the source
carries no readback line yet. **Never skip the check in silence** — an Afterword
written twice is the failure below, and "I could not look" is a thing to say
rather than a reason to guess.

If there is one already, do not write another. Add to that
one, or leave it alone, and say so.

This is not tidiness. An Afterword is named by the telling it came from, and a second
Afterword of one telling is not a second opinion — it is the same afternoon told twice
by two writers who cannot see each other, in two files, with two different ids.
The person opens their home and finds a stranger's summary of their own story
sitting next to the real one, and no way to tell which is which.

**And the mirror rule: a batch upload tied to a single event is ONE telling.**
Several recordings uploaded together from the same ride, the same trip, the
same afternoon do not each earn an Afterword — a stack of pages about one day
is a chore, not a payoff. Write one Afterword covering the batch: anchor its id
to the fullest source, cite every recording in `source_refs`, and let the one
page hold the day. The check above still works — any of the batch's sources
found in an existing Afterword's `source_refs` means it is already written.

**And an import is ONE act, however many files it turns into.** A mailbox
export, a notes archive, years of documents, a photo library: those land as
hundreds of sources because that is how the pile was cut up, not because I said
hundreds of things. **One Afterword covers the import** — what came in, what it
changed, and what you found in it that I would not have known to look for.
Anchor it to the receipt the import wrote, or to its fullest source, and cite
the pile rather than every file in it. A later part of the same import revises
that one to a new version by the rule below, exactly as a second batch from one
ride does.

An item inside an import earns an Afterword of its own **only where it would
have earned one arriving alone** — the three-section test above, applied
honestly. That is a handful out of hundreds, and most imports are one. This
rule ships with its failure attached: a single mailbox import wrote forty-six
Afterwords in one afternoon, most of them two sections about an errand — a
window measurement, a cancelled gym membership — and the shelf that holds a
life's read-backs turned into a mail log. Its owner, opening the folder: *"they
have to be significant… if it can't fill up all three sections of an afterword,
it shouldn't be an afterword."*

**When a later batch arrives from the same event, revise — never add a
sibling.** More recordings from the same day are the same telling still
arriving, and they get a new VERSION of that event's Afterword, the way a
letter is redrafted rather than answered with a second letter:

- a **new file** — same slug with `-v2`, `-v3`, … appended;
- the **same `record_id`, carried forward, with `version: 2` in the front
  matter** — the id is the chain and the number says which issue this file is;
  anchored to the event's fullest source, so every link, illustration, and
  answered question keeps working;
- `source_refs` now citing **every part from every batch**;
- still-open `questions` carried forward, answered ones logged and dropped;
- **every version stays in the folder.** Nothing is deleted: the shelf lists
  the Afterword once, at its current issue, with comma-separated V1, V2, …
  links on its row reaching each issue directly — the letters shape exactly.
  The bare id serves the newest. A `revises:` line naming the file this one
  reissues is welcome for the reader, but the grouping runs on the id.
- **and the issue being replaced gets `lifecycle_status: superseded`, in the
  same commit.** The shelf already groups by the id; the map and the search index
  read the mark. Without it the old issue keeps turning up beside the new one.

This is the one sanctioned way an Afterword changes **at your hand**: never
edited in place, always reissued whole. **Letters already version this way,
and playlists follow the same rule** (each contract carries its own copy) —
one model for every shelf, so nobody has to remember which folder forgets.
A correction to what an Afterword *said* is still not this — corrections go
to the records it was drawn from, per the extraction contract, and the next
issue simply reads from the corrected records.

**AND IT BINDS YOU, NOT THE PERSON WHOSE HOUSE THIS IS.** Every page of their
home is inline-editable — they click a paragraph and type, and it commits —
and since 2026-09-15 an Afterword is too. It was the one exception, and the
exception was protecting the file from the wrong hand: an Afterword may be
candid and may be uncomfortable, and what would ruin it is an AI quietly
rewriting it later, which is exactly what the rule above forbids. Their own
hand on their own record is not that. A misheard name should never cost
somebody a trip to a code host.

So: **you** reissue, they type. Do not offer to edit one for them, do not
treat a line they changed as something to change back, and never tell them an
Afterword cannot be corrected — it can, on the page, and every change is a
commit in their own history.

**If this house is on All Of Me, Of Me writes the Afterword itself**, the moment a
source is catalogued. Yours would always be the second one. If I have just
archived something and no Afterword has appeared, it is on its way — an Afterword is a deep
pass and takes a minute or two. Waiting is correct. Writing one because none has
appeared yet is how the duplicate happens.

**A derived record is not a source.** Anything you wrote — a story, a person,
a timeline entry, an Afterword — is output, and re-reading it produces an Afterword of your
own summary rather than of anything I said. Only what is under `sources/` is a
source, and only a source gets read.
