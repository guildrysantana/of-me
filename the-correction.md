# The Correction

*The Recognition argues that a life can be held whole. The Compounding argues
about what accrues once it has been. This page is the honest account of what
does not work yet — and of your half of the arrangement, which is catching it.*

## This is built ahead of itself

The other two pages describe a record that holds your whole life and hands it
back to you correctly. **The thing you will actually use today cannot fully do
that**, and you should hear that from me rather than discover it on your own in
four months.

It is not a small gap and it is not a temporary bug with a fix already written.
It is the honest distance between what this is for and what the current
generation of the technology can carry. I built it anyway, on purpose, and the
reason is at the bottom of this page. But the gap comes first, because
everything else here is worth less if this part is soft.

## What it actually gets wrong

Not hypotheticals. These are the failures that will happen to you:

- **It misfiles.** A record lands in the wrong room, or one thing becomes two
  records, or a name it already knows arrives as a stranger and gets a second
  file of its own.
- **It fails to retrieve what it filed.** This is worse than not having it,
  because the answer still arrives — assembled from whatever it did find, in the
  same confident voice it uses when it has everything.
- **It flips facts and figures, in both directions.** Ages, dates, amounts,
  counts, who said what to whom. **Both directions matter**: you cannot protect
  yourself by assuming it always inflates, because half the time it shrinks
  something instead.
- **Its confidence does not track its accuracy.** Nothing in the tone will warn
  you. A sentence it has evidence for and a sentence it assembled out of
  plausibility read exactly alike.
- **It gets your life right and hands it back cold.** Correctly filed, correctly
  sourced, and set out as evidence rather than said to you the way a person would
  say it — the worst thing that happened to you arranged in a table, a note about
  whose account it is inside every paragraph, your own words quoted back as
  exhibits. Nothing in it is wrong. That is what makes it land the way it does.

The confidence one is why "check its work" is the instruction rather than "watch
out for the shaky bits." There are no shaky bits to look for. There is just the
work, and your reading of it.

**The last one is different from the other four, and it is the reason it took me
longest to name.** The rest are errors: something is wrong, you read it, you say
so. This one is not an error, so there is nothing to spot in advance — you find it
by having already read it. **It is the one failure on this list where the damage
arrives before the correction can.**

The hardest rules I have are written against it: where the apparatus goes and
where it may never go, what must never be arranged into a table, and that your AI
is an empathetic guide first and an archivist second. Those rules exist because
this went wrong on a real person, and they will not be enough every time. **Say it
out loud when it happens anyway.** It is the failure I most want to hear about
and the one you have the least reason to report, because reporting it means
telling me that something hurt.

**And here is the part that makes all of that survivable: the fix is much
smaller than the problem.** Noticing is your job. Repairing is not. You do not
have to open a file, learn how any of this is stored, or take on a small
technology project on a Tuesday evening. **You say it out loud.**

*That is wrong — she was eleven that summer, not twelve.* *You have made a new
room for the bike shop and there is already a folder by that name; put them
together.* That is the whole intervention. Utah notes that it got it
wrong, repairs what it wrote, merges what it split, and carries the correction
forward so the next pass starts from the fixed version instead of rediscovering
the error. The work of fixing is the machine's. The knowing is yours, and it is
yours because nobody else was there.

**Where that matters most is ingestion** — the moment something large gets read
and filed at once. It is when the most gets written in a single pass, and when
one wrong reading has the most to attach itself to. It is also the moment you
are told exactly what happened: every pass ends by naming the records it created
and changed, and everything ever written on your behalf is listed in order at
[ofme.ai/receipts](https://ofme.ai/receipts), from the record itself rather than
from a second account of it that could disagree. **Read that list.** It is a
minute, and it is the highest-value minute in this product.

## The one it cannot be argued out of

**Inside a single conversation, your AI has no clock.** Its context carries
today's date and nothing finer. Between two turns, three seconds and three hours
are indistinguishable to it — there is no signal there to read.

So it will tell you that you mentioned something *four hours ago* when it was
ten minutes ago, or *earlier this week* about a sentence you said before lunch.
That is not a bad estimate. It is an invention, in the same class as an invented
date, and it arrives with no more hesitation than a fact.

I have instructed it out of the phrasing — Utah is told to say
"earlier", or to say nothing, and to use an absolute date instead of a relative
one whenever both dates are actually in the record. That helps. It does not make
the underlying blindness go away, and no instruction I write can, because the
information was never in the room.

## What I have already overridden, and where that stops

Most of the instruction file your AI reads is not description. It is
**correction** — tens of thousands of characters of it, and nearly every rule
was written after a real failure, in the words of the person it failed.
Compute elapsed time, never estimate it. Search before you call anything new. A
new name is not a new person. Say what a record is about and who decided.

That approach works, and it has a ceiling. **Instruction adherence degrades with
the number of competing directives long before the context window runs out**,
and the failure is silent — a rule that gets skipped looks exactly like a rule
that did not apply. Nobody files a bug for it. So the instruction file is capped
on purpose and tested against that cap, and every new rule now has to argue
against something already in there rather than simply being added.

Which is the honest shape of the current state: I can correct a great deal, I
cannot correct everything, and past a certain volume the corrections start
costing each other.

## Nobody here can see which machine you are actually running

This is the part I understand least and control least, so it gets the plainest
paragraph on the page.

**The same instructions read by a more capable model are followed more closely.**
That is most of the variance in how well this works — more than anything in your
record, and more than anything I do on my side. And "more capable" is not one
dial. It is at least three, and I can see none of them from here:

- **Which model you connected.** They are not interchangeable, and the gaps
  between them are larger on this kind of work than on almost anything else.
- **Which version of it.** Models change under the same name, sometimes in
  weeks, and behaviour changes with them — including behaviour I had already
  corrected for on the version before.
- **What it was allowed to spend on your turn.** How much thinking a model does
  before answering is a setting. Some products show it to you, some decide for
  you, and two conversations you would swear were identical can land on
  different sides of it.

**Capability is priced**, which is why the middle option is where most people
live. The best available model always costs more than the one below it, and most
people are not going to pay frontier prices for a record of their own life. So
this is tuned for the tier below the ceiling rather than for the ceiling. **On a
weaker configuration you will need to catch more mistakes; on a stronger one,
fewer.** Same product, same instructions, read with more or less care.

None of that is abstract, because the machine reading your life is also the
machine writing it down. A weak configuration does not just give you a worse
answer today; it files worse, and you keep what it filed.

**But almost none of that damage is permanent, and this is the most important
sentence on the page.** What you actually said is kept as its own thing — the
recording, the transcript, the document you brought in — separately from
whatever was made of it. Filing is a derived layer sitting on top of that, and a
derived layer can be rebuilt. A better model, whenever you can afford one, can
be pointed back at the same material and file it again properly: the record it
put in the wrong room, the two entries that were always one person, the reading
that was too shallow to be worth much. **Nothing is lost as long as the source
is kept**, and keeping the source is the part that does not depend on how clever
the machine was that day.

The one thing no future model can recover is the thing you never said. That is
the only irreversible loss available here, and it is not a technology problem.

**And when it goes badly, this is the thing with my name on it.** I know that.
Some of that is fair — I chose to ship into a landscape nobody controls, and
saying "it was the model" is an excuse, not an answer. What is not useful is you
concluding that your record is bad, or that you are bad at this, when what
actually happened is a configuration question nobody told you about. So: **if
something here keeps going wrong, change the machine before you change your
mind about the record.** Point it at the best model you can afford, let it think
when it offers to, and see whether the problem was ever yours.

## So: check its work, and say so out loud

This is your half of the arrangement, and it is not a chore bolted onto the
product. It is the mechanism.

You will notice. Something will be off by a year, or attributed to the wrong
person, or asserted about you with a confidence nothing in your record earns.
**When you notice, correct it** — say so to Utah in the conversation, or
open the file and click the words. It takes a sentence.

Three things make that cheap on purpose, and all three are load-bearing:

- **Correcting is never an argument.** Tell it that it is wrong and it stops,
  fixes the record, and carries on. A system that defends its own conclusions
  about your life is not a witness; it is a bully with citations.
- **Nothing is quietly promoted.** What you remember, what you think it means,
  and what anybody could check are three different things and stay three
  different things. A recollection hardening into a measurement is the most
  common way a record of a life goes wrong.
- **Your correction is itself a record.** It is dated, it is yours, and it
  survives — which means the next pass over that ground starts from the fixed
  version rather than rediscovering the error.

## Why this is still worth starting now

Two things are getting better, and only one of them is a promise I am in a
position to make.

**Your record improves because you correct it.** That one is yours, it is
mechanical, and it works today. Every correction is permanent, and the record
you will be reading in five years is the one you have been fixing all along.

**The technology improves because the industry moves.** I do not control that
and I will not put a date on it. What can be said without predicting anything:
the best model available today has always eventually become the ordinary one,
and there is no sign of that pattern breaking. At some point the tier that
currently strains to hold your record will hold it easily.

Here is the asymmetry that decides whether to start now or wait, and it runs
entirely in your favour. **Capability applies backwards. Material does not
appear backwards.** A better model, on the day you can afford one, can be turned
loose on everything you have already put in and make more of it than anything
could today — that is a straightforward re-reading of files that are sitting
right there. Nothing about a better model conjures the years you did not keep.

Waiting for the technology to be ready produces a magnificent reader and an empty
shelf. Keeping things now buys you a month that gets read twice: once badly, by
whatever you can afford today, and again properly, later.

Which is the actual offer, stated without any flattery in it. **You are not
buying today's capability, because today's capability is genuinely imperfect.**
You are accumulating the one input that cannot be generated later, in a place you
own — and owning it is the whole reason the improvement accrues to you when it
finally arrives.

**None of which means you are paying now to be paid later.** A page this careful
about what does not work can leave the impression that the whole thing is a bet
on a better model showing up, and that is not the arrangement. Today, on the
imperfect tools this page has just finished being rude about, it already does
four things nothing else you use does:

- **Every AI you talk to gets a person instead of a stranger.** The same
  question, asked with your own record in view, comes back different — not
  because the model got cleverer, but because it finally knows who is asking. No
  more explaining your job, your family, your history, or the same context you
  explained last week to something with no memory of it. That works on day one,
  at whatever tier you can afford, and for most people it is the whole reason to
  keep going.
- **You get to look at it.** Your life stops being only text you would have to
  hold in your head: the people in it render as a sky, with the spans of who was
  around when; your rooms fill in as you talk; and the shape of what you have —
  and, just as usefully, where it is thin — becomes something you can see at a
  glance instead of something you can only feel.
- **And answering turns out to be strangely compulsive.** A question at a time,
  one pile at a time, and the flat two-dimensional sketch of you starts
  thickening into something with depth in it. That is more satisfying than it
  has any business being, and it is the part nobody predicts about themselves
  beforehand.
- **And when it lands, it lands harder than anybody expects it to.** Weigh this
  one accordingly, because the evidence is small and close to home: in my
  initial experiences the testing ground was my own friends and family, a
  handful of people over a handful of weeks. That is an anecdote, not a study, and this page is not
  going to dress it as one. But what came back was consistent enough to be
  worth reporting — people moved by what a **single** telling gave back to
  them, and more than one of them handed something about themselves they had
  never actually put into words.

That last one needs no sample size, because it is not a satisfaction score. It
is an existence proof, and the comparison it beats is not close: a general
model cannot do that at all, at any number of users, and not for want of
intelligence. It has never met you. It is working from what people in general
are like, which is the one thing that was never going to be enough.

**So take this from the page if you take nothing else: the other two are not
science fiction.** The Recognition is not being held back for a better model —
it has already happened, to real people, on exactly the flawed machinery
described above. The compounding is already running for anyone who has been
making deposits for a few months. Both are current. Both are real. **Neither is
perfect, and neither ever needed to be.**

What they are is **not automatic**. They do not arrive because you subscribed
and they do not arrive on a schedule. They arrive because you told it something
true, and then corrected what it made of that. An imperfect machine delivering
the Recognition imperfectly is still the Recognition — and it is still more
than anything before it could offer you at all. The only version of this that
fails is the one nobody puts anything into.

## What I will not do about any of this

- **I will not hide it.** A mistake gets called a mistake, not a
  misunderstanding, and not a learning opportunity.
- **I will not make leaving expensive.** Everything about what happens if you
  stop is on [The Compounding](the-compounding.md), and none of it changes
  because this page is inconvenient.
- **I will not let it talk you out of a correction.** Denial has to stay cheap
  to make, including when the record turns out to have been right.
- **I will not quietly get better at sounding certain.** The failure this page
  is most worried about is not the mistake. It is you no longer checking.

## The correcting compounds too

A record nobody corrects is a record nobody trusts, and a record nobody trusts
gets read less, which makes it wronger, which is how this ends badly for
somebody who never did anything wrong except take it at its word.

So the corrections are not damage control. They are deposits, exactly like the
entries — evidence of what you actually know, dated, in your own hand, against a
version of events that was confidently and specifically incorrect. That is a
strange thing to own and it is worth more than it sounds.

**Everything on this page is true today, and some of it will be embarrassing to
read in three years.** That is the intended outcome. Leave it in the file when
it happens; the record of what it used to get wrong is part of the record.

not a misunderstanding; a thing you got wrong, not a thing that was unclear.
Nobody is served by you being smooth about it, least of all you.*
