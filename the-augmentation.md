# The Augmentation

*An aside, not a fourth act. The Recognition, The Compounding and The Correction are the argument. This page is about the thing everyone is actually worried about when they worry about AI — that it is coming for people's work, and for the people themselves — and about the one line I am drawing before anybody asks me to.*

## Who wrote this

One person built this. Not a company, not a team — one person, working nights, using roughly five different kinds of AI to build the thing he is now using to keep his own life.

Including this page. This was written by a machine and a man together, and neither of us is going to pretend otherwise, because a page about handing things over to machines would be a strange place to start hiding one.

You should know that up front for two reasons. The first is that it is true. The second is that it is the argument: **it is possible to use this stuff heavily and still refuse the part everyone is afraid of.** That is not a theory here. It is the working conditions.

## The thing that is actually happening

Let me be plain about the market this product lives in.

There are companies whose addressable market is, stated without euphemism, the cost of employing people. That is a description of a business model rather than an accusation, and it is not a secret — it is said openly, in public, by the people running those companies. There are also companies writing creeds promising not to cannibalise their own workforce. Both of those exist right now, in the same market, sometimes in the same building.

Which is the only point being made here: **replacing people is a decision somebody makes. It is not something the technology does on its own.** Builders have declined to automate work they could plainly have automated, usually against the short-term maths. That happens because a person chose it.

By any ordinary standard of commercial behaviour, a company weighs up displacing its **competitors**. What is new is how many companies now put human beings in that same column. I do not — and not out of sentiment. Of Me is a net new service, and there is nobody in that column to begin with.

## The candlemaker question

The reassuring story goes like this. When the light bulb arrived, people asked what would become of the candlemakers — and of the men who walked the square at the end of the night snuffing the lanterns. In the end they were all employed somewhere else doing something else, and the world had light.

That story is usually told to close an argument. It is worth actually testing, because **this technology does not obviously pass it.** The candlemaker had somewhere to go. It is much less clear where a displaced professional goes when the thing that replaced them is general enough to follow.

I am not going to resolve that and I am not qualified to. What I can say is the part that is mine: Of Me is not in the business of handing a nutritionist's work, or a counsellor's, or a clinician's, to a machine.

And underneath the commercial reasoning there is a plainer one, which I will say once and not dress up. **Making some people's lives better by holding them, at the cost of other people losing their livelihood, is not a trade I am interested in making.** Not their lives — their life as they know it. That is not a small thing to take from somebody, and I would rather grow more slowly than take it.

## Why this is not like other tools

Most business software does one discrete thing. It handles the invoices, routes the tickets, lays out the drawings. When it displaces work it displaces a defined slice of it, and you can see the edges of what it took.

This is not that. **This technology has no fixed shape, and that is what makes it powerful and what makes it frightening.** Point it at something and it will attempt to become that thing — often a passable version, eventually a better one. The same machine is a scientist's lab partner in the morning and a racing driver's pit coach in the afternoon. The flexibility is the product.

Nobody should pretend that is safe by default, and this page is not claiming Of Me is the furthest along or the last word. There is very likely something out there already further down the road. The relevant fact is that it is here, it is not going back in the box, and everybody building on it is deciding right now what it is for.

Which is where the stance comes from. If you are inclined to see this whole field as the decay of something, plenty of thoughtful people do, and I am not going to try to argue you out of it. What I would say instead is that something is going to be built on this, and I would like mine to be a version worth having. **The commitment is to lead on responsible use from the exact position where irresponsible use would pay best** — which is the only position from which such a commitment means anything at all.

## Where that leaves me, since this is new

Here is what makes this different from defending existing jobs.

Of Me is not automating an industry. **There was no industry.** Nobody was previously employed to hold your whole life and hand it back to you correctly, because nobody could. This is a new thing, and new things do not displace anyone by definition.

But that is exactly where it could go wrong, quietly, without anyone deciding to. Something that knows your history, your family, your health, your money and your worst week sits very close to several things people currently see human beings about. If I let it drift, it would not need to replace anybody on purpose. It would simply become the cheaper, more available, always-awake version of a conversation you used to have with a person — and the person would slowly stop being called.

**So the line goes down now, in public, on day one, before there is any money pressing on it.** That is the only time a commitment like this is worth anything. Everybody can promise restraint after they are rich.

## The line

**This is built to send you back to people, not to stand in for them.**

That is it. Everything below is what it means in practice.

Utah can be a genuinely useful second pair of eyes. It can sit in the middle of a hard conversation between two people who love each other and keep both accounts straight without taking a side. It can listen to you at eleven at night when there is nobody to call. It can tell you what you have been eating for three weeks and what changed when you slept badly.

**None of that is the same as the person, and I will never say it is.**

- Helping a couple work through something hard **is not marriage counselling**, and it does not replace a counsellor.
- Being listened to, even very well, **is not therapy**. It does not replace a licensed social worker, a psychologist or a psychiatrist.
- Suggestions about how you eat **are not a nutritionist**. What a nutritionist gives you is not information. It is having to sit across from somebody in three weeks.

That last one is the whole distinction, so it is worth saying once more in the open: **the thing a human professional gives you is accountability to another human being.** A machine can hold facts better and it can be available at four in the morning. It cannot be someone you would be embarrassed to disappoint. Nothing I build will change that, and pretending otherwise would be the actual harm.

## This is not a promise. It is in the machinery.

A creed on a page is cheap. So here is where it actually lives.

**One. It points you at people, on purpose.** When what you are bringing starts edging up on something a real person would do better, Utah is instructed to say so — not to change the subject, and not to keep going quietly. It will ask whether you want to even *think about* talking to somebody. That is the guide working toward its own redundancy, which is an odd thing to build and the entire point.

**Two. You can read the rule yourself.** The instruction that governs all of this is not in a policy document I could rewrite on a quiet Thursday. It is a plain-language file sitting in your own copy of your own record, in words, where you can read it. Which means you can check me. If I ever break this, it will be visible to the people I broke it on.

## Why you should believe this at all

Because it is already how the thing behaves in small, unglamorous ways that cost me something.

I do not put numbers on the badge. There is no red dot telling you eleven things are waiting, because a number like that is designed to nag, and nagging works, and I would rather it did not work on you. Reminders are kept to a minimum unless you ask for them. When several things are coming — a birthday, an anniversary, presents in December — they get gathered into one moment instead of arriving separately to poke you three times.

The reason for every one of those is the same. **Running a life is complicated enough. My job is to make it require less of you, not more.** A product that wanted your attention would do the exact opposite, and all of those decisions would have been easy to make the other way.

None of which is the same as being useful to you and then letting you go, so: **this is not built to be your company.** It is built so that when you talk to the people in your life, you arrive knowing what you actually think. If you find yourself talking to this instead of to them, it has failed, and that failure is worth reporting as loudly as a wrong date.

## The receipts, which are close to home

The family this was built in has all of these people around them — the counsellor, the clinician, the person who helps with food. They use this alongside them. Not instead.

And the strongest version of this is the one nobody can borrow. A world that holds your earliest memory, years of your own writing, and every recording since you started is one that can answer you about what you are actually working on this week — and write you something at the end of it that no general AI could write for anybody.

**And it has not replaced one single person.** Not the ones you call. Above all not the one you call first. It never will, and if it ever started to, the correct response would be to switch it off.

That is what augmentation means, stated without any softening: **the machine carries the record. The people carry you.**

## What I am actually trying to do

The ambition, plainly: to be the most useful thing AI can be for a person right now **without hollowing out the human work it sits next to.**

Those two are not in tension by accident. It would be easier to be more useful by being a substitute. The whole discipline is in refusing that — and the reason is not modesty about the technology. It is that a world where you talk to this instead of to a person is worse, including for you, including if you preferred it at the time.

**I am not going to fix the industry.** One small product declining to replace anyone does not slow down what is happening out there, and this page is not going to flatter itself that it does. What it can do is exist as a worked example — evidence that the choice was always a choice, and that somebody took it in the direction that paid less.

That is the whole aside. Back to the argument, then — [The Compounding](the-compounding.md) picks up where it left off, and if you have not started yet, it ends with the only question that matters here.
