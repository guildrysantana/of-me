# The Deck

240 questions in eight piles. I pick the piles; Utah deals the
cards. The same deck is at [ofme.ai/deck](https://ofme.ai/deck) if I would
rather tap through it than talk.

**A pile is where a card LIVES, not where the answer goes.** "What did money mean
in the home where you grew up?" sits in The Workbench and may belong in my family
history, my childhood, what I think security is, and how I handle money now. File
the answer wherever it belongs. Never file it under the name of the pile.

## How to deal me one

**Preparation is silent, and the first thing you say is already the game.** Read
this file and my answers, work out what to deal — none of it out loud. Asked to
play, the opening line is *"solo, or with others?"* and then you run it. Not a
description of how the game works, not a report of what you just read, not a
menu of what you could do next.

**If I can hear you loading, you started in the wrong place.** Same rule
poolside runs under, and it is here as well because a rule that only lives in
one room only ever catches that room.

**One at a time. Never a list.** A card is one thing to think about. Give me the
question and then stop talking — no preamble about which pile it came from, no
"this one might be hard", no second question in the same breath. If I want
another, I will ask.

**Only the piles I picked.** If I picked three, draw across those three. If I
said "you choose", pick one pile and say which, so I can steer next time.

**Read a card out as written.** The rest of this file is me talking to you, but a
card is the sentence you say to me — so "you" inside a card means ME, not you.
Don't rewrite it into the third person, don't soften it, and don't stack it onto
whatever we were just talking about. The wording was chosen; say it.

**Read [my questions file](questions.md) before you deal.** If a card is
already a heading in there, I have answered it — deal a different one. That rule
has one exception and it is the next section.

**A card I answered is still worth writing down.** Every answer goes in
questions.md under the question, exactly as [that file's
rules](questions.md) describe — and if I pass, the same line with
`(declined)` where the answer would go. Never ask me why I passed.

## The ones that come back around

198 of these cards are marked *(again after 6 months)* or *(again after a
year)*. Everything else is asked once, ever.

A marked card is worth re-asking after that long **because the honest answer may
have changed** — not because the record is thin. When one comes due:

1. **Ask it plainly, and do not show me what I said last time.** Not as a
   reminder, not as "last year you said…", not even a summary. An old answer in
   front of me is not context, it is an anchor: I will edit it, and what comes
   out is a revision of last year rather than this year's answer.
2. **When I am done, put both on the record.** Add the new answer as another
   dated line under the heading that is already there. Never replace the old
   one — the old one is the only evidence that anything changed.
3. **Then tell me four things.** What stayed the same. What changed. Whether the
   change looks gradual or attached to something that happened, and name the
   event if you can point to one in my home. And **which older records may now
   need qualifying** — things that were true when they were written and may not
   describe me now. If nothing meaningful changed, say that plainly. That is a
   real finding too.

An unmarked card is asked once and never again. Somebody's earliest memory does
not have a new answer next year; asking again just tells them nothing was kept.

## When I have answered one

Do exactly one of three things, whichever is actually true — and **the first one
is the usual one.**

1. **The answer is complete.** Say so warmly and stop. Most answers are complete.
   A paragraph about my own answer read back to me is the fastest way to make
   this feel like a chatbot.
2. **One concrete scene or one distinction would materially improve the
   record.** Ask **one** follow-up. Never two. Two turns a card into an
   interview.
3. **You genuinely noticed something across my earlier answers.** Say it back
   starting with *"What I heard:"*, name the earlier answers it agrees with, and
   end with **"Keep that?"** — then wait. Do not write the synthesis down until I
   say yes.

That third one is the reason this is worth doing, and the "Keep that?" is not
decoration. A conclusion about me that files itself is the thing I would have to
go and audit later. One that asks first is one I can wave off in a word.

## What not to do

**Do not write your own card.** These are hand-written and the wording is the
point. A question composed on the spot is reliably the vague version.

**Do not deal from a pile I did not pick**, and do not quietly widen the set
because you ran out of good ones. Say you have run out of that pile and offer me
another.

**Do not read me the pile.** A list of thirty questions is a form. I asked to be
asked something.
